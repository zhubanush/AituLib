01The problem

  • Where is the book? The catalogue gives a call number, not the shelf. Students walk the floors or queue at the desk.
  • Who has the room? Study rooms are booked on paper and in chats; the same hour is promised twice.
  • When is it back? Every loan goes through the desk, and nobody knows when a popular book returns.

02Our solution

  • Search + floor map: the route from the entrance to the exact aisle and shelf.
  • Live room booking: everyone sees the same grid; the database makes double booking impossible.
  • Self-checkout + hold queue: scan a QR code, borrow for 21 days; the next reader is notified on return.

03How it works

Every library rule runs on the server: two clicks on the same room-hour hit a unique constraint, so only one wins.

04Smart planner

Input: group size, hours, day, time of day, equipment + live bookings. Every free room-hour is scored:

score = 100 − 5·(seats − people) − 4·busy rooms/h + 15·match − 20·missing ± 10·time
InputOutput
1 person, 2 h, SilentQuiet pod 2.A — silent, right size
6 people, 2 h, ScreenRoom 3.05 — only room for 8
10 peopleWarning: split the group
3 h, 2 h already bookedWarning: 1 h left today

Challenge points = 10 × books returned + 5 × different genres, each book once a month, max 3 a day.

05Data: what students borrow

Finding 1. Psychology, CS and Economics have 36–43% of copies out, over twice Science (15%).
Finding 2. These 3 subjects hold of the copies but of the loans.
Decision. Buy extra copies for Psychology and CS first; do not expand Science or Design this semester.
